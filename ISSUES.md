# Open issues and ideas

*2026-08-23, after four device sessions. Kept separate from
[ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), which is about what to build; this is about what's
wrong, what's unproven, and what we've learned that changes the plan.*

---

## 1. The model's value, measured

The question raised after the third session — "we're likely not accruing much
value from the model, especially against the effort" — deserves a precise answer
rather than agreement, and measurement gave a different one than expected.

**What was measured.** Model takes sit 0.10–0.50 away from what the deterministic
path composes from the same template, on a seven-axis profile (density, offbeat
share, rest share, step/skip/leap shares, mean note length). At the time, the
nine templates sat **0.04 apart from each other** — median of 36 pairs, max 0.07.

So the model's output was five to ten times *more* distinct than the templates
were from one another. The similarity being heard was real and its cause was the
opposite of the obvious one: every template composed the same line, so everything
sounded like everything, including the model's takes — which were the most
distinct thing in the session.

That is now fixed (median 0.10, max 0.28; density spreads 1.5–5.7 notes per bar
where it was flat at 2.0). **The question should be re-asked by ear now that the
deterministic path actually differentiates.**

**What the measurement doesn't cover, and this matters.** `PatternProfile` scores
seven axes and *none of them is contour or pitch content*. Two lines can differ on
every measured axis and still both be "an ostinato that walks up the scale". The
observation that the model produces "something as generic as the template, likely
with the same type of contour" is about an axis nothing here measures. Adding a
contour measure — interval-class distribution, direction-change rate, degree
bigram entropy — would let that be checked instead of debated.

**Specific model failures observed**, none yet reproduced in a test:

- A generated pattern with an empty first bar that wasn't counted as a rest.
  `ensureBreathing` and `capDeadAir` both reason about gaps *between* notes; a
  leading silence has no note before it, so neither sees it. Probably a real bug
  with a small fix.
- Ostinati at any temperature. Partly alleviated by variety scoring, which
  annotates but never rejects (Open Question 6, settled deliberately).
- Comping generated by the model is faster than lines — fewer events — and its
  results were close to the templates for the same reason lines were.

**The honest position.** Foundation Models cannot be trained on device
([TRAINING.md](TRAINING.md) §1), and prompt-level conditioning is most of what
remains. Deprioritising it is a legitimate answer. Before deciding, two things are
worth trying because they're cheap: generation parameters we haven't varied
(sampling mode, and whether a different seed per chunk changes anything), and the
one use whose economics clearly work — authoring templates rather than takes,
which costs one request once instead of ~1.8 seconds per note forever.

**On template authoring.** It works and it is a *finite* win, which the gate makes
visible: sweeping 72 characters across density, air and syncopation, 9 land
somewhere the nine existing templates don't already cover — **12%**. Four
consecutive attempts in a device session were refused as too close, which is the
gate doing its job rather than failing. Expect a handful of genuinely new
templates and then saturation.

## 2. The deterministic generator worth building

The strongest idea to come out of the sessions, and it doesn't need a model.

**A constrained random walk over chord tones**, with passing tones mostly on weak
beats and occasionally on strong ones for tension. Everything it needs already
exists: `ChordSymbol` carries chord tones, scale, tensions and avoid notes;
`MelodyPatterns` places degrees against whatever chord is sounding;
`MelodyGeneratorSupport.fold` handles register; `ChordVoicings.lead` shows how
voice leading is done here.

Why it's likely to beat both current paths:

- **Against the model:** instant, free, and it can be *made* to respect voice
  leading rather than asked to.
- **Against the gesture grammar:** the grammar composes shapes and then fits them
  to harmony. A walk is harmony-first — it decides the next note from the current
  chord, the previous note and the metric position, which is closer to how a line
  is actually improvised, and it produces contour variety the grammar's fixed
  contour vocabulary can't.

Sketch of the state, which is deliberately the same shape as `MelodyChain`'s:
current degree, metric position, whether the last note was a chord tone, and the
direction of the last move. Transition weights from chord-tone/colour/avoid
membership, with step preferred over leap, resolution after a leap, and a
tension budget that allows a non-chord tone on a strong beat occasionally rather
than never.

This should be built and compared against the gesture grammar on the same
measurements, not assumed better.

## 3. Fixed since the last session

- **Variants flattened comping.** Twice. First because they went through
  degree extraction, which is one note per onset by construction. Then because
  they re-comped from the figure name and discarded the take's own material — so
  exploring a model comp offered deterministic comps instead. Variants now keep
  the parent's hits and re-lay the chords under them.
- **"Instant · no model" produced lines in Chords mode**, which made the mode
  look like a setting only Generate honoured.
- **Templates didn't differentiate** (§1).
- **A third-party Apple Intelligence extension** was breaking generation, and the
  first diagnosis of that was wrong — see F12b in the roadmap.

## 4. Known and unfixed

Ordered by how likely they are to bite.

1. **The interface has outgrown its structure.** Sixteen top-level sections in a
   2,700-line view. Every feature arrived as another section, which is how it got
   here. Structural rather than a wording problem — see §5.
2. **No contour measurement.** §1: the axis the complaints are actually about
   isn't measured, so claims about it can't be settled.
3. **A leading silence isn't treated as a rest.** §1.
4. **The learned models are recomputed on every draw.** O(takes × notes) on the
   main thread, per button press and per interface refresh. Fine at fifty takes,
   not at five hundred. Storing them (S4) fixes it.
5. **`StyleLearner` measures comping takes as if they were lines**, so a corpus
   with comps in it reports nonsense — 57% "leaps" in one session, which were
   simultaneous voices.
6. **Takes still can't be named.** `retitle` exists and nothing calls it.
7. **`partial` aspects are recorded and unused.** "The rhythm works" should be a
   transform; the vocabulary was chosen for it.
8. **The fit report is computed, tested and invisible.**
9. **The eighth-note grid is the binding constraint** (D1). No triplets, no swung
   triplet feel.
10. **The library is `UserDefaults`, not an App Group** (I5/L4).
11. **`PatternStore` and the learned models have no export or import.**
12. **`previousTakeID` is encoded but not decoded.**

## 5. For the design pass

The interface problem is not that any control is badly named. It is that
**capability grew as sections** and nothing was ever removed or subsumed, so the
view is a stack of sixteen equals with no hierarchy between them.

Current top-level sections, in order: progression · next take · instant ·
transport · shape · stored lines · feel · drift · current take · listen · review ·
variants · history — plus three conditional banners (status, model-down,
diagnostics).

What a design pass would need to know:

- **There are two loops, not one.** *Playing* (transport, feel, drift, the roll)
  and *deciding* (review, variants, history, curation). They are interleaved
  down the view and want separating.
- **Six sources of material is the feature, and thirteen buttons is the
  symptom.** Model, stored line, composed phrase, learned draw (two kinds),
  variant, morph, captured, comp. They differ in *what they cost* (instant vs
  ~1.8s/note) and *where they come from* (somebody else's vocabulary, a grammar,
  your own material, a language model). Either axis is a better organising
  principle than the current one, which is the order they were built in.
- **Some sections are settings and some are actions**, and they look identical.
- **What's genuinely modal:** Line vs Chords changes what most controls mean, and
  currently changes only two of them.
- **What's rarely touched:** progression settings, rotation membership, drift,
  listen, template authoring. These are correct to hide; the question is behind
  what.
